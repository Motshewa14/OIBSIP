Mocha (Caffè Mocha)

R55

A latte with chocolate syrup or cocoa.

☕ Espresso 🍫 Chocolate 🥛 Steamed Milk
📖

Origin: Its name comes from Mocha, Yemen, a historic port that was the global hub for coffee trade from the 15th to 17th centuries. Yemeni beans naturally had a chocolatey note, so Europeans later recreated that flavor by adding actual chocolate. Today, it's a sweeter, dessert-like cousin of the latte.

Cappuccino

R50

Espresso with steamed milk and a thick layer of foam.

☕ Espresso 🥛 Steamed Milk 🫧 Foam
📖

Origin: The word "cappuccino" literally translates to "small hood". It was named after the brown-robed Capuchin friars of 16th-century Italy, whose distinctive hooded habits matched the rich, reddish-brown color that dark coffee turns when mixed with milk.

Irish Coffee

R65

Hot coffee with Irish whiskey, brown sugar, and thick cream.

☕ Coffee 🥃 Whiskey 🍬 Brown Sugar 🍦 Cream
📖

Origin: Created in 1940s Ireland by chef Joe Sheridan at Foynes Port, he added whiskey to warm up weary transatlantic seaplane passengers. A San Francisco journalist later brought the recipe to the US, making it a cocktail classic.

Frappé

R48

Instant coffee shaken with sugar, water, and milk into a frothy iced drink.

🧊 Iced ☕ Instant Coffee 🥛 Milk 🇬🇷 Greek
📖

Origin: The accidental Greek invention: Instant coffee, sugar, water, and milk shaken into a frothy iced drink. Invented in 1957 in Thessaloniki, Greece, at a trade fair—a Nestlé rep couldn't find hot water, so he shook instant coffee with cold water and ice in a shaker. It became a national summer obsession and a global symbol of Greek café culture.

Latte

R52

Espresso with lots of steamed milk and a layer of foam.

☕ Espresso 🥛 Steamed Milk 🎨 Latte Art
📖

Origin: The American "translation" that Italy hates. Order a latte in Italy and you'll get a glass of plain milk (latte means "milk" in Italian). The modern café latte—espresso with lots of steamed milk—was popularized in the 1950s by Italian-American cafes in cities like San Francisco and New York. But the real kicker? It was Seattle's coffee culture (pre-Starbucks) in the 1980s that turned it into an art form, introducing latte art. The first recorded latte art photo? 1989 in a Seattle café.
